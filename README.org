#+title: org-moor
#+options: toc:2 num:nil

Bring what drifts past into your own harbour, and tie it to a heading.

* Why

Most of what a day hands you arrives in pieces: a chat message with no
background, a mail whose minutes are an attachment, a link to a file on a
shared drive, a manual of two hundred pages of which three matter.  The
places they arrive in are poor places to keep them.  Their search does not
find what you remember, and the names people give files and folders do not
help it.  What you can rely on is your own environment.

Bringing a thing in is not the end of it.  A file kept is only data; it
becomes information when you have read it and written down what it means,
in sentences you would stand behind.  And information is only worth having
if you can get back to it -- to what you wrote, to the file it came from, to
the place in that file, to where the file was in the first place -- in
seconds, whenever you are asked, however long it has been.

So this package does five things, and none of them is to organise:

| Step        | What it does                                                                     |
|-------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Take        | bring a thing in when you meet it, which is usually outside Emacs                |
| Fathom      | write what it means, in a capture-like buffer, starting from a draft of its text |
| Tie         | join what you wrote to the source, and to the place inside it                    |
| Return      | get back to the heading, to the copy, to the place, to the origin                |
| Re-tie      | move a source to another heading, gather, or let a copy go                       |

What the package fixes is the structure that holds these together:
properties, blocks, and where copies are kept.  What you write and how you
arrange it under a heading is yours; there are no prescribed sections.

* Words

Five words carry the weight, and they are kept apart on purpose.

- a *heading* is always one of yours, in any of your Org files.  The
  package writes into it; it does not decide where it lives.
- a *source* is something you took in: a web page, a file, a message.  It
  has an /origin/ -- where it was when you met it, a URL or a path -- which
  is kept even after the origin changes or disappears.
- a *copy* is a source's bytes, kept in the store.  A source may have no
  copy (a URL you only link to) or lose it (a copy let go); its origin and
  what you wrote about it remain.
- the *store* is the directory copies live in, one directory per source,
  named by the source's id.  Nothing in it is meant to be browsed or
  arranged by hand: it is reached from Org, the way a database is reached
  through its queries, which is what spares you from filing.
- a *place* is a position inside a source -- a page, a slide -- that a note
  points to.

A *note* is what you write.  It is plain Org under the heading, and the
package does not look inside it, except to find where it points.

* Structure

** A source is a heading

Each source is a heading of its own, usually under the heading it belongs
to, with these properties:

| Property        | Holds                                                    |
|-----------------+----------------------------------------------------------|
| ~ID~            | the source's id; also names its directory in the store   |
| ~MOOR_ORIGIN~   | where it was: a URL, or the path it was taken from       |
| ~MOOR_VIA~      | how it arrived: ~web~, ~file~, ~mail~, ~chat~, ...       |
| ~MOOR_FILE~     | the copy's file name in the store, if it has one         |
| ~MOOR_TAKEN~    | when it was taken, an inactive timestamp                 |
| ~NOTER_DOCUMENT~ | the copy's path, when org-noter can read it             |

~NOTER_DOCUMENT~ is written so that an org-noter session opens on the
heading as it is; notes org-noter writes there, with their ~NOTER_PAGE~, are
places like any other.

#+begin_example
,* Expense approval, new rules
,** Approval manual 2026
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:             6f1c...
:MOOR_ORIGIN:    https://example.sharepoint.com/.../manual.pdf
:MOOR_VIA:       chat
:MOOR_FILE:      manual.pdf
:MOOR_TAKEN:     [2026-10-17 Sat 10:12]
:NOTER_DOCUMENT: ~/notes/source/6f/1c.../manual.pdf
:END:
Approval goes to the department head before finance. [[moor:6f1c...::12][p. 12]]
#+end_example

** Words taken as they are

A message is quoted, not filed.  Its title -- who sent it, about what -- is
written on the block's first line, where Org allows parameters, and the
time it was taken follows:

#+begin_example
,#+begin_quote Chat from Robin [2026-10-17 Sat 10:12]
Can we keep the old form for this month?
,#+end_quote
#+end_example

** Places

A place is written as a ~moor:~ link: ~moor:ID::PAGE~.  Following it opens
the copy at the page; without a copy, the origin.  A ~moor:ID~ link with no
page is the source itself.

** The store

~org-moor-store-directory~ holds the copies.  Keep it next to your Org
files, so that they travel together, and out of everyday search: a store of
PDFs and slides makes a text search slow, and is better searched on purpose,
with a tool that reads such files.  ~org-moor-store-directory~ is that root.

Images that are part of what you write -- a screenshot, a figure -- are not
sources: they have no origin elsewhere and belong with ~org-attach~ as
before.
